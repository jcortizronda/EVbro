# EVbro manual: every screen and what it does

*Versión en español: [docs/manual.md](../manual.md).*

EVbro is your Ebro car's companion: it shows what the car tells the Chery cloud, sends it commands, keeps its history (trips, charges, fuel-ups, garage visits, expenses) and alerts you about what matters. It is designed for the phone (bottom dock with **Home, Charging, Comfort and More**) and on a computer it turns into a sidebar with every section in view. Available in Spanish, English and Portuguese (Settings → System → Language).

**Signing in:** with your EVbro username and password, or with your fingerprint if you added it. If you forget the password and the server sends e-mail, «Forgot your password?» sends a link to your account's email (it expires in 30 minutes and works once); otherwise the administrator sets a new one.

One rule holds across the whole app: **what you see comes from the car**. If a value has not arrived, you see "No data", never a made-up value or a default "closed".

## Home

The front page of the selected car (the car selector is at the top if you have several).

- **Header with the photo** of the car, its name, number plate and state: *Asleep*, *Driving*, *Charging*… The coloured dot says whether the data is recent.
- **Battery and petrol**: percentage and range of each (a pure EV has no petrol; a combustion car has no battery).
- **Car figure** seen from above: open doors and tailgate swing outwards, open windows and sunroof are painted amber, the padlock says whether it is locked, with the cable connected a **hose** runs from the charging port to a charging gun, with the power and the time the charge will end under it (or «Not charging»; tapping it opens Charging), and each wheel carries its pressure. Below, one line sums up what is open. **Tapping a part opens its action** (windows, boot, sunroof, lock).
- **Action tiles**: Lock, Find (lights and horn), Boot, Windows, Sunroof, Locate car and Alarm (its state, on or off, in the top right corner). The title says what it is and the state only *Open/Closed*. The sunroof can be opened, closed or **vented** (tilted; it then says *Tilted*). **Lock** always opens a menu with the current state and both orders, *Unlock the car* and *Lock the car*, so a stray tap never opens it. Whatever **opens** the car asks for the Ebro PIN on every send (configurable, see Settings → Account). If the Ebro cloud answers that **your car does not support** a control (the heated rear window on some S900s, for instance), the button is dimmed with «Not allowed»: it stays there and you can try again.
- **Map**: the last known position. Folded, it is a fixed preview; a tap expands it full screen. **Centre** goes back to the car and **Go to** opens Google Maps to it. Below it, the address and, when the car is inside one of your **geofences**, its name («Home · Some street»). The position comes from the cloud (it does not wake the car); **Locate car** does ask the car for a fresh fix. If the cloud keeps an old position while you drive, or the car parks far from its last point, EVbro sends it by itself with the saved PIN (without a saved PIN it does not).
- **Tyres (TPMS)**: pressure and temperature of each wheel.
- **Refresh** button (top right): queries the cloud now. It does not wake the car.

**Customise home** (button at the bottom of the page). Each block is a *widget* you can **move** (dragging by the ⠿ handle or with the arrows), **remove** (×) or **put back** with «Add widget». The header always stays at the top. Besides the usual blocks there are new widgets: **Next maintenance** (the nearest service or task and the MOT), **Last trip**, **Last charge**, **This month's spending**, **Scheduled charge** (battery target and schedule), **Climate** (temperatures and turning it on or off) and **Consumption** over the last 30 days. Tapping one opens its section. While you edit, the buttons do nothing, so no command is sent by mistake. The layout belongs to **your account**: it looks the same on the phone and the computer, and every person with access has their own. «Reset» brings back the usual home screen. Widgets your car cannot show (live data without an Ebro account, charging on a petrol car) do not appear. On a computer the map always sits in its own right-hand column.

**Charging** and **Comfort** are customised the same way, with «Customise charging» and «Customise comfort» at the bottom of each page: you can reorder or remove their blocks (battery and target, figures, charge control, charge history, scheduled charge, charge detail; climate control, glass and steering wheel, seats, scheduled climate) and, in Charging, add the last charge, this month's spending or consumption. Each page keeps its own order and «Reset» only affects that page.

**App icon shortcuts.** With EVbro installed on the phone, press and hold its icon: **Lock the car**, **Turn on the climate**, **Charging** and **Trips** appear (each one can be dragged to the home screen as an icon of its own; their names are in Spanish). The command ones never send anything on their own: they open the app and ask first, naming the car. EVbro cannot put a widget with data on the Android home screen, because Chrome does not allow it for web apps.

**Bottom bar.** On the phone you choose what goes in the bottom bar: Home and More always at the ends and up to 4 sections in between (6 icons at most), from Charging, Comfort and any of the sections in More. Change it with **Customise bottom bar**, at the end of More: add, remove and reorder with the arrows. It is saved in your account, so it is the same on all your phones; «Reset» goes back to Charging and Comfort. Whatever you put in the bar no longer appears in More, so it is not there twice; remove it from the bar and it comes back to More. A computer has no bottom bar (everything is in the sidebar).

## Charging

*(Called «Energy» until 01-11-2026.)*

Everything about the battery and charging.

- **Current charge** and **battery target** (50–100 %, in steps of 5, with the slider). The target is kept in EVbro, not in the car: when it is reached while charging, EVbro **pauses** the charge and resumes it on its own when plugged in with the battery 5 points below. That pause needs the **PIN saved** in Settings → Account: without it the web reminds you next to the slider and, if the car does not accept the pause when the target is reached, you get a notice («Could not pause the charge») instead of charging on without knowing. While the charge is paused at the target, EVbro reads the car every 5 minutes to learn early that you plug it in; if the car's live channel is listening, every 30 is enough, because the cable arrives through it at once.
- **Charge control: Stop / Start**. The car does not allow stopping or starting a charge directly; EVbro does it through the car's own **scheduled charging** (it schedules a window that already ended to stop, and removes it to start). A "How it works" panel explains it on the screen itself.
- **Scheduled charging**: start and end time (or duration) to charge every day in that window (off-peak tariff). It is written to the car.
- **EV consumption · 30 days**: kWh/100 km of the last month's trips. Only trips of 2 km or more without petrol and with measured energy count, the same rule as the Trips summary and Statistics: on a very short drive a single battery point and the odometer's whole-km rounding give a meaningless figure.
- **Battery data**: power, voltage, current and time left while charging, with **the time it will finish** («2 h 50 min, until 16:49»; «tomorrow 06:10» past midnight), also on the Home charging bar. With a target below 100 %, also **how long until it reaches it and at what time** («To the target (80 %): about 1 h 51 min, around 15:50»): an estimate that shares out the car's own time in proportion to the points left, as if the power held steady.
- **Charge history** (tabs *Session curve* and *History*): each session with place, kWh into the battery (estimated from the battery points gained) and, if you enter them, the kWh from your meter, the real **efficiency** and the cost. The cost comes from the **tariff of the geofence** where you charged (fixed, free, 2.0TD or PVPC) or you type it with **Add cost** (**Edit cost** once it has one): enter the **total paid** or the **price per kWh** and EVbro works out the other. That price per kWh may come out a little higher than the charger's: the charger bills the energy it delivers and EVbro counts what reaches the battery, which is less because of charging losses; with the meter kWh it is worked out over those. In the history, unfolding a charge lets you mark it as AC or DC fast.
- **Charges by hand** (in the charge history, «Add a charge» and «Import»): to write down the charges from before you used EVbro, or one that was not recorded. You fill in what you know: **charger kWh**, **price per kWh** and **total paid** (with two of the three the third is worked out; 0 if it was free), the **battery charged (%)**, the date, the time, the type (AC or DC) and the place. A note below the fields says how it will be counted: with the charger kWh (or the total and the price) the charge goes **by the charger side**, the cost is worked out with them and, if you also give the %, EVbro estimates what went into the battery and the **efficiency**; with only the % (and the price) it goes **by the battery side**: EVbro estimates what went in from the car's capacity and works out the cost on that, without efficiency. The percentage is never worked out from the kWh. In «Where» you choose one of your geofences (with a fixed or a free tariff it suggests its price), a place you typed before or «Another place…». Without a date, the charge is filed as earlier than your first day in EVbro: it counts in the totals, but not in any particular month. **Import** takes an Excel (.xlsx) or a CSV file with one charge per row, with the columns called whatever you like and the same rules: EVbro shows how it understood them and what will happen to each row (ready, already there or to review) before saving anything, and the whole import can be undone. There is an example template to download. They show in the history with the **Manual** tag and can be corrected and deleted; they have no charge curve, so they are not part of the battery health. The manager and the linked accounts allowed to edit can add them. **In the totals and the costs** each charge counts with the charger kWh when known (the manual ones, and the detected ones whose meter kWh you entered) and otherwise with what the car measured: an estimate of the energy paid for. On a detected charge, entering the meter kWh recomputes its cost (also when the price comes from the geofence) and its efficiency.

An honest note: charge control through scheduling is built following the official app's protocol, but **it could not be validated in every situation against the real car**. If something does not add up, report it.

## Comfort

What can be switched on from the sofa.

- **Climate control**: on/off with set temperature (16–30 °C) and presets *Cool* (16 °C), *Comfort* (22 °C) and *Warm* (30 °C). Turning it on sends the chosen temperature.
- **Glass, wheel and air**: six buttons.
  - *Demist*: air to the windscreen. It turns the climate control on too.
  - *Heated windscreen* and *Rear window*: the heated glass.
  - Heated *Steering wheel*.
  - Air *Purifier*. It turns the climate control on too; when you turn it off, the climate control stays on.
  - *Quick heating*: in one go, the climate control at 30 °C, the four seats, the steering wheel and the heated windscreen for 15 minutes. Turning it off turns everything off.
- **Seats**: heating and ventilation per seat.
- **Scheduled climate**: the server turns the climate control on at the time and days you choose, even with the app closed. It needs the PIN saved in the account.
- **Scheduled battery preheating** (the official app's «Schedule departure»): you choose the time you leave and the weekdays, and the car warms the battery from an hour and a half before; it is best left plugged in. The schedule lives in the car: EVbro shows the one it has, with its state (**On** or **Off**, as the car reports it), and lets you change the time, the days and the switch with «Schedule». It needs the PIN saved in the account.
- Every button shows the car's real state; after a command it shows "waiting for confirmation" until the car confirms.

## More

The menu with the remaining sections. On a computer, the side bar groups them as **History** and **Manage**.

### History

**Statistics.** Charts of the history with a period picker (12 months, this year, last year, all…): kilometres per month (in green those driven on electricity and in amber with the petrol engine, from the car's EV odometer), energy per month (kWh and litres), spending per month, cost per kilometre, electric consumption per month (only of trips that used no petrol: in those that do, the engine recharges the battery and the consumption would come out lower than it really is), battery health (usable capacity **measured** from the power curve of every charge of 20 points or more, and how it compares with the sheet's capacity), consumption by speed, where it charges and drain while parked (battery the car loses while parked).

**Timeline.** Everything recorded about the car in one list by date (trips, charges, fuel-ups, garage, expenses, documents, geofence arrivals and departures), with filters by type. At the top, the **global search**: a part number, a garage, an invoice or an address, across all your cars. Tapping an item takes you to its screen.

**Trips.** Each detected drive with origin and destination (address), odometer kilometres, duration, battery and petrol used and speeds. A drive EVbro never saw (the car was asleep between two readings) is marked **Unseen**: the kilometres and the consumption are real, the times approximate, and it has no route. **See route** opens the map with the track, which can be **replayed** and downloaded as **GPX**. Filters by geofence, place, minimum distance, speed and period. The **period summary** adds up every complete trip; its average consumption in kWh/100 km is computed only from trips of 2 km or more without petrol and with measured energy, as in Charging and Statistics, so the same period gives the same figure. **Frequent places**: where the car ends up most often; a geofence can be created from there. **Journeys**: groups, between two dates, the drives, charges, fuel-ups and expenses of a holiday and tells you what it all cost and used. Trips are detected on their own from the car's data; incomplete ones (no end) can be corrected by hand.

**Fuel-ups.** Real petrol consumption: litres between fill-ups, cost per 100 km, price per litre, how many days and km between fuel-ups. Fuel-ups are detected on their own when the tank level rises (with **estimated** litres); once you enter the receipt litres they are no longer marked as estimated. Add any missing ones (**Add fuel-up**) and enter the receipt price: of **litres**, **price per litre** and **total paid** two are enough, the third is worked out; the time is optional. **Import** brings the fuel-ups from before EVbro from an Excel (.xlsx) or a CSV file, with one fuel-up per row and the columns called whatever you like: each row needs the date and the litres (or the total and the price per litre) and, if you have them, the time, the odometer, the petrol station, whether you filled the tank and notes (without that column, all of them count as full). As with charges, EVbro shows how it understood the columns and what will happen to each row (ready, already there or to review) before saving anything, the whole import can be undone and there is an example template. A fuel-up in the file that matches one you already have (same day and similar litres) is not brought in again. The **Petrol station** field suggests the ones you have used before, and when you fill up where you already did, the name is filled in for you. Tick **Full tank** when you fill up: between two fill-ups the consumption is exact.

**Nearby petrol stations** (at the top of Fuel-ups): the stations within 5 km of the car with the price of the fuel you choose (95 E5 by default; also 95 Premium and 98), on a map —the 10 cheapest with their price; green the cheapest, red the dearest— and in a list from cheapest to dearest. Tapping one shows it on the map with its address, opening hours and «Directions». If you move the map, **Search this area** looks there; **Around the car** goes back. The prices are those of Spain's Ministry for the Ecological Transition (Spain only) and may differ from the pump. When EVbro **detects a fuel-up** at one of those stations, it takes its name and that day's 95 E5 price, so the cost comes out on its own (with estimated litres until you enter the receipt ones).

**Maintenance.** Two plans: the **official EBRO** one (the maker's schedule: 15,000 to 90,000 km, with what is replaced and inspected at each service and the official workshop time; each service is due 15,000 km or 12 months after the previous one recorded) or a **custom** one with your own tasks and intervals in kilometres or months. The **ITV** (roadworthiness test) is worked out from the registration date (or from the sticker you enter). Record each **service**, **one-off job** (pads, 12 V battery…) or ITV with date, kilometres, garage, cost, notes, the **parts fitted** and the **invoice** as PDF or photo; a wrong record is deleted with the bin in the history (if it was an official service, it becomes pending again). Maintenance alerts go out through the channels in Settings → Alerts.

**EVbro recommendations** (in Maintenance). Checks that are not in the brand's plan and depend on how you use the car: **brake pads and discs** (every 15,000 km or 12 months; a plug-in hybrid hardly uses them and the discs rust), **old petrol** (4 months without a fuel-up), **rotating the tyres** (every 10,000 km; 8,000 if you drive mostly on fast roads), **age of the tyres** (at 5 years and then every year), **12 V battery** (at 3 years and then every year) and **wiper blades** (every 12 months). When one is due you get a notice (the ones already due when EVbro first looked are listed without a notice) and answer it right there: **Checked, it is fine** (it counts again from that day and those km), **Record intervention** (opens the usual form with the name filled in) or **Remind me later** (30 days or 1,000 km). Each one can be switched off. They are EVbro's advice with approximate intervals: they do not replace the official services.

**Expenses.** Insurance, taxes, garage, tolls, fines and other payments, by category. For recurring ones (insurance, tax) you set the **next time** and EVbro reminds you 30 days before, in the last week and when it is due. The **insurance** also reminds you **60 and 45 days** before: in Spain a policy renews itself unless the insurer is told a month ahead, so there is time to compare.

**Costs.** The balance of everything: purchase, charges, fuel-ups, maintenance and expenses; total since purchase, running cost, cost per 100 km and by category, with a monthly chart and the period picker.

### Manage

**Cars.** The list of your cars (tap one and the whole app switches to it) and the **sheet** of the selected one:
- *Details*: name (on a car with an Ebro account it is the **car's name in the official account**: saving it changes it in Ebro too, and if Ebro does not accept it the old one stays), make, model, version, **colour** (on an Ebro, a list of its model's official paints, or «Other…» to type it; the car drawing on Home is painted with it), environmental badge, number plate, chassis number, registration and delivery dates, purchase price and kilometres, **insurance** (company, policy, cover, assistance phone, renewal), **documents** (registration certificate, technical sheet, policy, purchase invoice…: uploaded and opened from the app), **tyres** (sizes and reference pressures for the alert) and **energy** (usable battery, tank and engine type; changing the battery recalculates every trip and charge).
- *Consumables*: what your car is fitted with (oil, filters, pads, tyres, wiper blades…), with make, **brand ref.** (the number given by the part's own brand: Mann, Bosch…) and **OEM ref.** (the car maker's own part number: Ebro's on an Ebro), so you know it straight away at the garage. It updates on its own when you record a job with parts. You can **propose** a part to the shared catalogue, also while adding it («Save and propose»); the administrator reviews it before publishing it to the other owners of the same model. With **Show online catalogue** the list shows, next to your parts, the approved ones in the catalogue for your model; each of your parts says what state it is in (*not proposed*, *proposal sent*, *in the catalogue* or *rejected*). Tapping a catalogue part copies it to your car, and if you already had one of that kind it warns you that it will be replaced.
- **Car book (PDF)**: a report with the sections you choose (sheet, maintenance, trips, charges, fuel-ups, expenses, geofences, documents) to keep, print, take to the garage or hand over with the car. Create it from the **Car book** card in Cars, from **More** or, on a computer, from the sidebar. It has a cover with the car (your photo if you uploaded one), the number plate and its key data, a contents page with links, and on every page the EVbro header and «Page X of Y». The charges section includes the measured battery health and the trips section the share of electric km since new. It comes out in your language.
- **Add a car without an Ebro account**: a car of any make to keep its maintenance, expenses, costs and parts (no live data). With a GPS tracker and a Traccar server it also gets position and trips.
- **Photo** of the car, used in the Home header.

**Geofences.** Areas on the map (home, work, charger…) with a radius. They serve three purposes: **alerting** on arrival or departure, **pricing** the charges made inside (fixed tariff, free, 2.0TD with its three prices or regulated PVPC) and classifying trips. With 2.0TD the periods follow Spanish time (Canary time in that zone); Saturdays, Sundays and the fixed-date national holidays (1 and 6 January, 1 May, 15 August, 12 October, 1 November and 6, 8 and 25 December) are off-peak all day. Regional holidays, holidays moved to a Monday and Good Friday do not count, as CNMC Circular 3/2020 sets out. If you create a geofence where you had already been, the earlier charges and trips there take its name; **their prices do not change** (only new charges take the tariff). The **Arrivals and departures** card is the log of every pass through them.

**Settings.** Each part is a collapsible section; the phone remembers which ones you left open.
- *Account*: your EVbro user (change password, sign out, and **your email**, used only to recover the password: changing it asks for your password and the previous address is told), **fingerprint sign-in** (asks for your password, then add this device and sign in without a password; requires HTTPS), the **Ebro account** (connect, sync cars —«Sync» warns if the cloud rejects the saved password—, save or change the **PIN saved in EVbro** (only the copy EVbro uses for commands), **change the Ebro PIN** —the 6-digit PIN of the account (Ebro checks the current PIN; it does not need to be saved), in the Ebro cloud; if a PIN is saved in EVbro, it becomes the new one—, **change the Ebro password**, or **reset the password or the PIN** if you do not remember them (from the two change dialogs: a slide-the-piece captcha, which Ebro asks for, and a code by SMS to the account's phone; if it does not arrive, «Request another SMS» turns on after a minute) —the one of the official app: EVbro changes it in the cloud, keeps the new one and stays connected—; if the Ebro cloud stops accepting the session, EVbro signs in again by itself with the saved password and, if that is rejected too, it notifies you and the header says «Session expired» until you connect it again) and **when to ask for the PIN**: only when opening the car (default), always or never. **Your data**: download everything as a ZIP and import a ZIP on another server. A link to what data this server keeps. **Delete the account**: deletes your account for good, with everything the server keeps of it (cars with their history, linked accounts, documents and photos); to confirm you type your username and your password. Your car and your Ebro account do not change. The dialog carries an **optional** survey (why you are leaving and a comment): if you answer, your answer is kept with your username and read by the server's administrator; it is the only thing left of the account. Download the ZIP first if you want to keep a copy.
- *Sharing*: linked accounts (partner, family) and what each can do with each car: view; view and edit; view and control; view, edit and control.
- *Alerts*: notifications on this device (Web Push) or Telegram, and which events alert: geofence arrival and departure, fuel reserve, faults, windows left open, low battery, charge completed or interrupted, maintenance, alarm, tyre pressure and due dates, with their thresholds. Each alert arrives **once** for as long as the situation lasts (an open window, the tank in reserve…), not every so often. «See alert history» opens the same history as the bell. The last switch, **Messages from the administrator**, is different: off does not discard them, it **silences** them (they are kept under the bell, but not sent to the phone or the other channels).
- *Connections*: the **car channel** (the live connection to the cloud: status, messages received, Reconnect button), Home Assistant's **MQTT** broker, so your cars appear there, and GPS position from a **Traccar** server.
- *System*: **appearance** (light, dark or automatic theme and accent colour: blue, pink, violet, green, amber or red; saved on the device), **language** of the account and installed version.

**Sensors.** All the data the car sends, as it arrives, with name and explanation, by category (cables and charging, battery, tyres, body, climate, engine and diagnostics) and a search box. Useful to understand what each signal means. Not every model sends the same things (the S900, for instance, does not report the cabin temperature): what your car has never sent is folded at the end, under «readings this car does not send».

**Alerts** (bell in the header): everything EVbro has told you, grouped by day with the newest first. A **red dot** on the bell means alerts you have not seen yet, on any of your devices; opening it marks them as seen and the new ones carry a «New» label. Alerts are kept here **even if you have no channel** set up, so none is lost. If an alert did not get through a channel, it says so underneath. To remove **one** alert, swipe it sideways, like the notifications on a phone; with a mouse, press its **×**. **Clear** removes them all. If this device can receive notifications and they are off, the bell offers to turn them on; it also suggests it once when you connect the Ebro account.

## How the data arrives

- The **poller** queries the Chery cloud every hour with the car parked, every 30 minutes plugged in, every 2 while charging and every minute while driving. Querying **does not wake the car**; **commands** do.
- The **car channel** (MQTT) receives doors, lock, cable and engine instantly, and confirms every command. It only works with the owner's account, and the cloud keeps **one live connection per account**: if you open the official app, the last one in keeps it. If another app takes it, the channel stops and EVbro **does not reconnect it by itself** (not even after a server restart), so as not to throw that app out; a notice on **Home** and the «EBRO account (official)» card say so, and the **account light** (top right) turns from green to **orange**; if what fails is the whole account (no account, or the cloud no longer accepts the session), it turns **red**: once you close the other app, press **Sync** (the button is in the notice itself), which reconnects the account and the channel too (or Settings → Connections → Reconnect).
- Trips and charges are **detected on their own** from that data; the kWh of trips and charges are estimates (battery points × sheet capacity). The litres of a detected fuel-up are too, until you enter the ones on the receipt.

## The admin console

It is another web app, on another port, for the local network only. The administrator has no cars and sees nobody's data: it creates **managers**, issues **invitations** (with their code and, when the server knows its public address, a **link** that opens «Create account» with the code already filled in), sets **limits** (cars, linked accounts, file space), tunes the **server** (how much history is kept, polling intervals), moderates the shared consumables **catalogue** (approve, reject, edit proposals, add parts already approved; connect to another server's catalogue) and reads a **log** of its actions and aggregate server figures. It is also in the three languages.

**Anonymous reports.** When the administrator is created (or, if it was not answered, the first time you sign in) the console asks whether you want to send the project one anonymous report a day: version, which features are used, how the Ebro cloud and the commands respond, and the logged errors. It helps find and fix bugs. It carries no names, users, plates, VINs, positions, trips or amounts, and **See what is sent** shows the exact report before you decide. Turn it on or off whenever you like in the Server tab.

**Reads and orders sent to the Ebro cloud.** In Statistics, a table with what the server asks of the Ebro cloud each day: reads (with data, empty and failed), single queries (alarm and charge plan), orders and an estimate of the requests. Everything leaves from the same address, so it shows the volume and lets you notice early if the cloud starts refusing.

**Messages to the accounts.** In the Messages tab the administrator types a title and a text and sends it to every manager or to the chosen ones, and to their linked accounts too if wanted: planned maintenance, news. Each account gets it under its notices bell, marked as a message from the administrator, and through the channels it has switched on; accounts that silenced them only see it under the bell. It is text only, goes exactly as typed (it is not translated) and cannot be taken back once sent. The console shows how many accounts each message reached, not who has read it.

**Why accounts are deleted.** In the Managers tab, below the list, are the answers left (if they wish) by managers who delete their own account: how many times each reason was ticked and, for each answer, the date, the username, the reasons, the comment, how many cars and linked accounts it had and how many days the account lived.

**The project's catalogue.** When the administrator is created you choose whether to use the project's shared consumables catalogue or only your server's (if it was not answered, the console asks at the top). To connect it later, **Connect to the project's catalogue** in the Catalogue tab is enough: the server signs up by itself at `catalog.evbro.app`, with no key to ask for. Pasting a key you were given (`ebc_…`) also works. You can go back to your own catalogue whenever you like.

## What EVbro does not do (on purpose)

- It does not start the engine remotely (the car refuses) nor "really" stop or start a charge: it uses the schedule for that.
- It does not keep your PIN in the browser; on the server it is encrypted and only if you want.
- It does not send your data to anyone: the server is yours. The only outbound traffic goes to the Chery cloud (to talk to the car), OpenStreetMap tiles, the alert channels you enable and, only if the administrator turns them on, an anonymous daily report to the project (no personal data).
